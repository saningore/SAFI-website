1. Executive Summary
The current SAFI website provides a strong conceptual foundation. It communicates a distinctive African-led focus on savanna landscapes and connects climate, health, food, peace, culture, indigenous knowledge and policy. The five-centre model and research-to-action approach are particularly strong institutional assets.
The next stage should not simply add more pages. The website should evolve into a credible, human, evidence-oriented public platform that clearly answers five questions:
Who is SAFI?
What does SAFI uniquely do?
Who are the people and institutions behind SAFI?
What work is SAFI undertaking or developing?
How can a researcher, government, community, funder, university or business work with SAFI?
Recommended institutional proposition:
SAFI connects knowledge, people and action to shape resilient savanna futures.
The live homepage currently presents SAFI as a Ghana-based, African-led research, innovation and policy institute focused on inland and coastal savannas, with five connected research centres and a research-to-action model. The strategy below retains those strengths while making the institutional proposition clearer and more engaging.

WHY SAVANNA FUTURES MATTER
Why Africa’s Savannas Matter
Savannas are living landscapes where people grow food, sustain livelihoods, exchange knowledge, protect biodiversity and build their futures. Grasslands, woodlands, rivers and wetlands connect people with nature, culture, health and economic opportunity. Changes in climate, land use, health and governance affect these relationships together.
SAFI studies these connections so that decisions in one area can account for their consequences in others.
WHO WE ARE
Who We Are
The Savannah Futures Institute (SAFI) is a Ghana-based, African-led organisation working at the intersection of science, indigenous knowledge, innovation, policy and sustainable development.
SAFI connects knowledge about landscapes, health, food, peace, culture and economies to help communities and decision-makers shape resilient futures.
WHAT WE DO

Research • Innovation • Policy • Partnerships • Capacity Development • Community Engagement

The existing circular SAFI mark is clean and adaptable. Build a complete visual system around it rather than repeatedly redesigning the logo.
Core visual idea: savanna systems, connection, regeneration and future.

12. Visual, Layout & Website Design Specification
This section translates the visual and brand direction into a practical specification for the website designer and developer. The aim is to make SAFI credible, African-led, contemporary, evidence-oriented, human and future-facing without making the website visually crowded.
12.1 Overall Design Direction
• Design character: modern African institutional, research-led, warm, confident, natural and future-facing.
• Visual principle: use the savanna as a living system rather than a decorative background. Images should show relationships between landscapes, people, knowledge, livelihoods, biodiversity and action.
• Avoid generic NGO imagery, excessive stock photography, busy gradients, excessive animation, clip-art, culturally inappropriate decorative patterns and crowded information blocks.
• Use generous whitespace, strong editorial hierarchy, authentic photography, restrained natural colours, clear cards, maps, diagrams and evidence-led visualisations.
• Brand personality: credible for universities, governments and funders, while remaining accessible to communities, young researchers and the wider public.

12.4 Global Page Layout
• Maximum content width: approximately 1,200–1,280 px on large desktop screens.
• Desktop side margins: approximately 5–7vw, reducing naturally on smaller screens.
• Use a consistent 12-column grid on desktop; collapse to fewer columns on tablet and a single-column layout on mobile where appropriate.
• Section vertical spacing: approximately 80–120 px desktop and 56–80 px mobile.
• Card internal padding: approximately 24–32 px.
• Use restrained 10–16 px corner radius; avoid excessive app-style rounding.
• Use subtle borders and shadows rather than heavy outlines or strong drop shadows.
• Every page should begin with a clear title/intro block and end with a relevant next action.

12.7 Alternative Images / Image Backup Plan
Where original SAFI photography is not yet available, use a controlled secondary image library rather than arbitrary stock photography. Every image should be checked for licensing, geographic relevance, cultural appropriateness and factual accuracy.
• Savanna landscape — seasonal grassland/woodland mosaic, river, wetland or agricultural landscape.
• Shea landscape — shea trees, shea parklands, women processing shea or community agroforestry.
• People and knowledge — farmers, researchers, community meetings, traditional knowledge transmission or youth innovation.
• Biodiversity — locally relevant plants, wildlife, wetlands or ecosystem interactions.
• Research and innovation — field sampling, GIS/mapping, health research, workshops or innovation demonstrations.
• Maps and diagrams — use a custom SAFI map or diagram where a photograph would be generic or misleading.
• If no suitable photograph exists, a clean data visualisation, map, illustration or typographic feature is preferable to irrelevant stock imagery.
12.8 Image Treatment & Technical Standards
• Use consistent aspect ratios: approximately 16:9 for hero/feature images, 4:3 or 3:2 for cards, and 1:1 for people profiles where appropriate.
• Use modern compressed formats such as WebP or AVIF where supported, with appropriate fallbacks.
• Prepare responsive image sizes rather than serving one very large image to all devices.
• Use focal-point cropping so people, faces, trees and important landscape features are not cut off on mobile.
• Apply light colour correction only; avoid heavy filters that alter the natural appearance of landscapes or people.
• Every meaningful image must have concise, descriptive alternative text. Decorative images should use empty alt text.
• Add photographer/source credits where required by licence or institutional policy.

12.12 Navigation, Buttons & Interaction
• Keep the main navigation simple: ABOUT | RESEARCH | PROGRAMMES | IMPACT | KNOWLEDGE HUB | PARTNERSHIPS | PEOPLE | CONTACT.
• Use 'Partner With SAFI' as the principal highlighted CTA in the navigation.
• Primary buttons: filled green. Secondary buttons: outlined green or text link with arrow.
• Use consistent CTA language: Explore, Learn More, Read, View Case Study, Download, Partner With SAFI.
• Avoid generic buttons such as 'Click Here'.
• On mobile, use a clean menu with the Partner With SAFI action still easy to reach.
• Use subtle hover states and short transitions; avoid distracting animations.
12.13 Mobile-First Layout
• Design mobile layouts first, then expand to tablet and desktop.
• Hero text should remain readable without covering faces or important landscape features.
• Stack cards vertically and avoid horizontal scrolling except for deliberate, accessible carousels.
• Maintain comfortable touch targets for buttons and links.
• Use accordions for long technical content where appropriate.
• Ensure tables, maps and data graphics have mobile alternatives or usable horizontal scrolling.
• Do not hide essential information on mobile merely to simplify the design.

12.15 Design Quality Control Before Launch
• Check the website at approximately 375 px, 768 px, 1024 px and large desktop widths.
• Check every page for consistent heading sizes, spacing, buttons and image crops.
• Check that all images have appropriate alt text and licensing/source records.
• Check contrast for text placed over photographs.
• Check that the same image is not repeatedly used across multiple major sections unless intentionally used as a signature image.
• Check that all five research centres have equal visual weight and consistent page structure.
• Check that the homepage communicates the SAFI story within the first screenful and remains scannable throughout.
• Check performance after images are uploaded; large images should not materially slow mobile loading.
• Conduct a review with at least one researcher, one non-specialist visitor and one potential partner/funder before final launch.

18. Final Strategic Recommendation
SAFI should resist the temptation to make the website simply bigger. Its strength is the connected institutional idea: savanna landscapes are simultaneously environmental, social, cultural, health, economic and governance systems.
The website should tell one coherent story: SAFI connects knowledge, people and action to shape resilient savanna futures.
The five research centres provide the intellectual structure; programmes demonstrate practical application; people and partners provide credibility; the Knowledge Hub provides evidence; and case studies demonstrate impact. This creates a clear journey from identity → research → action → evidence → partnership.
Recommended homepage closing line
Healthy landscapes. Healthy people. Peaceful communities. Resilient futures.
This line can serve as a memorable SAFI brand statement while the longer institutional proposition explains how SAFI achieves it.
