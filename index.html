Why savanna futures matter

Savannas are living landscapes where people grow food, sustain livelihoods, exchange knowledge, protect biodiversity and build their futures.

Grasslands, woodlands, rivers and wetlands connect people with nature, culture, health and economic opportunity. Changes in climate, land use, health and governance affect these relationships together. SAFI studies these connections so that decisions in one area can account for their consequences in others.

Who we are

The Savannah Futures Institute (SAFI) is a Ghana-based, African-led organisation working at the intersection of science, indigenous knowledge, innovation, policy and sustainable development.

SAFI connects knowledge about landscapes, health, food, peace, culture and economies to help communities and decision-makers shape resilient futures. From Accra, we build partnerships with universities, governments, traditional institutions, communities, civil society, businesses and international partners.

The Savannah Futures Academy is how that capability is built, connecting research with practice for researchers, practitioners, policymakers and community knowledge experts.

Active project

Savanna Conservation Movement (SACOMO)

Developing young savanna conservation champions

An environmental learning-to-action programme engaging basic and secondary school students in biodiversity conservation, ecosystem restoration and climate awareness. Through school clubs, practical field learning and community activities, SACOMO aims to develop a new generation of young conservation champions.

View project →
Active project

Circular Economy for Net Zero

Creating zero-waste savanna agrifood systems

An innovative dryland carrot production and value-chain model linking crop and livestock systems. Carrot residues are used as animal feed, while livestock manure is returned to farmland as organic soil-improving matter. The programme aims to reduce waste, strengthen soil health and generate sustainable rural livelihoods.

View project →
Active project

Savanna Biodiversity

Protecting transboundary elephant migration corridors

A conservation initiative focused on elephant migration along the Red and White Volta wildlife corridors. It proposes to combine AI-enabled monitoring with participatory citizen science and community stewardship to protect habitats, discourage poaching and reduce human–wildlife conflicts.

Impact

Evidence of change

SAFI measures impact through knowledge generated, capability strengthened, partnerships built, policies informed, innovations tested and community outcomes supported — using verified evidence and documented case studies.

Cattle

Pastoral herds connect savanna landscapes, nutrition and rural livelihoods. Evidence on mobility, grazing and market access helps communities and policymakers shape resilient food systems.

Ostriches

Large savanna birds reflect healthy open habitats and biodiversity. Conservation and community stewardship along corridors link wildlife, land use and sustainable futures.

Goats

Small ruminants support household income and food security across drylands. Research and programmes track how herd health, fodder and value chains can strengthen local economies.
